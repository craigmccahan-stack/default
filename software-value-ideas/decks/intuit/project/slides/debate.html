Four bear arguments, tested against the numbers

What bears say

What the data shows

Our read

AI agents will file taxes for free

AI costs ~$0.12 a return vs ~$162 of TurboTax revenue per return (Goldman). No AI entrant has disclosed real volume; TurboTax’s share of self-prepared e-files slipped ~2 points to ~59%.

Real pressure on DIY pricing; Intuit is cutting entry prices and offers free Credit Karma Tax.

Growth has peaked

FY27 revenue guided +9–10%, from 14%; the 3-year platform target was cut to 10–15% from 15–20%. GBS is still guided +13–14%.

Slower but double-digit, at a price that assumes 1.8%.

AI-native rivals will disrupt QuickBooks

Xero grew 31% with Melio; Basis and Rillet raised $100M each. Intuit: mid-market customers +28%; it is buying Sequence, an AI-native billing tool (15 Oct).

The fight is in the mid-market, where Intuit’s distribution is hard to copy.

FY26 cash flow was flattered

True: immediate R&D expensing cut cash taxes. Consensus FY27 FCF is $8.2B, down ~5%.

Even on normalised FCF the owner yield is ~7%.

Net: the bears are right that do-it-yourself tax is under pressure, and wrong that it defines Intuit.

Sources: Goldman Sachs via Yahoo (2 Jun 2026); IRS filing-season statistics; Intuit FY26 results and Investor Day; Xero FY26.
